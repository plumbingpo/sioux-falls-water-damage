Understanding Why Basements Get Wet

Choosing the right solution starts with identifying how the water is entering, because basements leak for different reasons. Hydrostatic pressure builds when saturated soil around your foundation pushes water through cracks, joints, and even porous concrete itself. Lateral pressure from expanding clay soil can crack foundation walls and let water in. If the yard slopes the wrong way, gutters are blocked, or drainage is inadequate, rain collects at the foundation and works its way inside. Finally, there is condensation: cool surfaces in a basement pull moisture out of warm humid air, leaving things damp although nothing is leaking.

Waterproofing From the Outside

Long term, nothing works as well as handling the water outside, before it reaches the wall. Exterior foundation waterproofing means excavating around the foundation, applying waterproof membrane coatings, and installing or upgrading the exterior drain tile. It is the most expensive approach, but it addresses the problem at its source and gives the most complete protection. One of the cheapest and most useful outside fixes is grading: the soil should drop a minimum of 1 inch per foot over the first 6 feet from the house.

Inside Fixes That Manage Water

Inside systems do not stop water at the wall; they control it once it has come through. Water that reaches the basement perimeter is caught by an interior French drain, which carries it to the sump pump to be discharged. A sump pump, preferably one backed up by a battery, handles the water reliably when storms hit or the power goes out. Interior waterproof coatings and sealants give some protection against minor seepage and efflorescence, but they do not replace proper drainage when water intrusion is significant.

Keeping Your Sump Pump Ready

A sump pump only protects a Sioux Falls basement when it is maintained. Every three months, fill the pit with water until the float trips and confirm the pump runs. Listen for unusual sounds that could point to mechanical problems. Basements flood most often when the power is out, so add a battery backup system. Consider a water-powered backup pump for extra redundancy. Make sure the discharge line sends water at least 6 feet from your foundation and has a check valve to prevent backflow.
