Whether the water came from a storm, an overflowing river, a backed-up sewer, a failed sump pump or a burst plumbing line, flood damage needs a trained crew right away. In nearly every case the water falls into Category 2, known as gray water, or Category 3, known as black water, which means contaminants, bacteria and other hazards that are a real health risk. Day or night, 24/7, Sioux Falls Restoration sends flood cleanup crews throughout Sioux Falls to remove floodwater, sanitize the affected rooms and safely restore your home or building.

Cleanup opens with rapid extraction: truck-mounted units and submersible pumps that pull thousands of gallons an hour out of the building. With the standing water gone, our IICRC-certified technicians check the contamination level and apply the right sanitizing steps, including antimicrobial treatment, HEPA air filtration and disinfection of every affected surface. Materials beyond saving, like contaminated drywall, damaged insulation and saturated carpet pad, are removed and disposed of the right way.

After extraction and sanitizing, we set up commercial drying equipment, including LGR (low-grain refrigerant) dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers, to dry the structure of your property thoroughly. Daily readings from calibrated moisture meters and a thermal imaging camera tell us when everything is fully dry, which is what keeps mold from growing. Every stage of the job is recorded for your claim: photographs, detailed moisture maps, logs of the equipment used and the scope-of-work reports that insurance adjusters require.

What a Professional Brings to Flood Cleanup

There is much more to a flood cleanup than removing the water you can see. Chemicals, bacteria, sewage and other contamination ride in with the flood and create real health dangers. Professional flood crews are trained in containment, personal protective equipment (PPE) and sanitizing protocols that keep your family from exposure to pathogens such as E. coli, hepatitis and other waterborne diseases.

Beyond the health concerns, a poor flood cleanup often leaves hidden moisture that turns into severe mold growth within days. With thermal imaging, psychrometric calculations and moisture meters, trained technicians confirm that subfloors, structural framing, wall cavities and every other wet area dry to an acceptable standard. Drying by measurement is the one dependable method for avoiding secondary damage, which can add thousands to the repair bill.

Maintenance Tips for Flood Cleanup

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Keep gutters and storm drains clear of debris so water can flow properly
  • Wherever flooding is likely, get appliances and electrical equipment up above floor level
  • Install backwater valves on sewer lines to stop sewage from backing up into your home
  • Add downspout extensions so roof water empties well away from the foundation
  • Keep documents and keepsakes in watertight bins on shelves, not on a basement floor
  • Add a sump pump with battery backup, since power outages and storms tend to arrive together

Warning Signs for Flood Cleanup

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Mud, silt or debris left on floors once the water goes down
  • Tide marks on drywall or trim where the water peaked
  • Roof or wall openings left by a storm, with rain pouring through
  • Ground so saturated that puddles sit against the foundation walls
  • Water flooding out of a dishwasher, water heater or washing machine
  • Basement flooding after the sump pump quit in a downpour
  • Water coming in through foundation cracks, window wells or door thresholds
