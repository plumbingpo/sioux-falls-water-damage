Bathroom Water Damage Help in Sioux Falls

A bathroom is designed to handle water on its surfaces, never below them. Once caulk splits, grout thins out or the toilet seal gives up, each shower and flush sends a bit of water through. Hidden from view, it builds up behind the wall tile and down in the subfloor. In Sioux Falls, we locate the entry point, measure how far the water has spread, then dry or take out the wet material.

These leaks are usually slow, so the damage is often deeper and older than it appears. Subfloor under a toilet can be soft all the way through while the tile above still looks fine. Our crew maps the wet area using meters and a thermal camera so nothing extra gets torn out, and only spots that cannot dry where they are get opened.

Signs to Look For

  • Vinyl flooring that curls at the edges or has discolored near the toilet or tub
  • A toilet that rocks slightly, or dark staining around its base
  • The ceiling beneath the bathroom shows a brownish ring or has started to droop
  • A warped or swollen cabinet floor under the vanity sink
  • Floor that feels spongy by the vanity or next to the tub
  • Tiles near the tub that have loosened, shift when stepped on, or sound hollow
Why Speed Matters

How the Damage Progresses

The first few days

Water Slips Through

With every shower, the bad caulk or grout lets a small amount of water through. It soaks into the backer board and the edge of the subfloor, with nothing visible yet.

The first several weeks

The Subfloor Never Dries

Showers come too often for the wood beneath the tile to ever dry. Within the wall cavity, and across the bottom of the floor, mold begins.

Months one to three

Signs Show Up

The caulk line goes black, the grout gets darker, and tiles work loose. A stain shows on the ceiling of any room below and slowly grows.

Six months and beyond

Structure Weakens

Subfloor around the toilet or tub turns soft and joists may begin to rot. At this point the job means new tile and carpentry on top of the drying.

Our Process

Our Bathroom Water Damage Process

1

Find the Leak

One by one we check the supply lines, toilet, shower and tub, then follow the water under the tile and into the walls with meters and a thermal camera.

2

Remove What Is Ruined

We take out rotted subfloor, soft wall board and tile that has come loose. Where the toilet leaked, we treat the area as contaminated and remove porous material that cannot be cleaned.

3

Dry the Cavities

Air movers and dehumidifiers dry the subfloor, the wall framing and the ceiling space below. We apply antimicrobial treatment and confirm dry readings before anything is closed up.

4

Rebuild and Reseal

We replace subfloor and backer board, and can coordinate tile, a new toilet seal, fresh caulk and ceiling repair so the leak path is closed, not covered over.

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Damage Downstairs

The stain on the lower ceiling can be several feet off from the leak itself, because water travels along pipes and joists first. Wiring and light fixtures may lie along that route.

Mold in the Tiled Wall

Neither grout nor tile is a waterproof barrier without help. When the layer behind them stays wet, mold grows on the wall board where cleaning never reaches.

Trapped Steam

A weak or missing exhaust fan leaves steam in the room after every shower. That moisture settles on walls and ceilings, feeds mold, and slows the drying of anything that is already wet.

Slow Vanity Drips

Months can pass with a supply valve or drain joint dripping, hidden by whatever is stored in the vanity. Water swells the cabinet bottom, then passes into the back wall and the subfloor.
