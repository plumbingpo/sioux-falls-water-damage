Leaking Pipe Water Damage in Sioux Falls: What to Know

A leaking pipe rarely announces itself. The drip is hidden in a wall cavity, a sink cabinet or beneath the slab, so the first clue tends to be a stain, a spongy patch of floor or an oddly high water bill. Often that means weeks of moisture have already gone into whatever surrounds the pipe. We serve Sioux Falls homeowners by tracing the full wet area and drying every bit of it, including what is out of sight.

Fixing the pipe ends the leak, yet the moisture already inside the wall or floor stays put. This is where our team comes in. We use moisture meters and thermal imaging to map the wet materials, open only what has to be opened, and set air movers and dehumidifiers until readings are back to normal. Because slow leaks give mold time to start, we also check for growth and treat affected surfaces.

Do This First

What to Do Right Now

  1. Shut Off the WaterWhen a toilet or sink line is dripping, shut the valve on the supply right at that fixture. When the source is unclear, the house main is the valve to turn off.
  2. Keep Power AwayTurn off the breaker serving the area when water is close to a light fixture, switch or outlet. Leave any wet cord or electrical device alone.
  3. Read the MeterShut off all taps and fixtures, then go look at the water meter. Movement on the dial means water is escaping out of sight, perhaps beneath the slab.
  4. Clear Out and Take PhotosEmpty the closet or cabinet by the leak, then photograph the pipe, any stains and any warped material before repairs start or items are discarded.
  5. Get a Moisture CheckAsk for meter readings of the area before anyone patches it closed. A wall that feels dry on the surface can still be wet inside the cavity.
Coverage

Does Insurance Cover It?

A slow leak and a sudden one are not handled the same way by insurance. Coverage is common for accidental, sudden damage, while a leak that ran for months or weeks is usually not covered and is specifically excluded in some policies. If the leak was concealed and you could not have known, it may be viewed more favorably than a visible leak nobody dealt with. Check your policy and ask your agent. So the adjuster can work from solid facts, we provide photos, moisture readings and notes on the leak source.

Pricing

What Affects the Cost

  • Any mold found, since it must be contained and removed before the drying can be completed.

  • Whether the leak lasted days or months, and the size of the area it wet.

The Usual Culprits

What Causes It

  • High water pressure that stresses joints and valves year after year until one weeps.
  • Drain fittings and P-traps below sinks that work loose and leak whenever water goes down.
  • A compression fitting or supply line connection at a sink or toilet that has loosened or worn with use.
  • A hairline split left by freezing weather in a line running through a crawl space or outside wall.
  • Hidden joints, soldered or crimped, that fail behind walls and above ceilings.
If It Is Left

What the Water Damages

Swollen Cabinets

Under a sink, a cabinet base made of particleboard puffs up and falls apart after it takes on water. They do not return to shape, so long leaks often mean replacing the base.

Decayed Wall Framing

After weeks of dampness, bottom plates and wall studs start to go soft and rot. Once framing lumber is no longer sound, it must be removed and new wood put in.

Ceiling and Drywall Damage

Leaks over a ceiling or behind a wall surface show as bubbling paint and brown stains. Wet drywall softens and sags over time, and those sections often have to come out.

Flooring Damage

Moisture from a leaking slab or wall line spreads out below the finished floor. The result is cupped hardwood, laminate with swollen seams, and vinyl or tile coming loose as its adhesive fails.
