Water Heater Leak Cleanup Help in Sioux Falls

A water heater holds 40 to 80 gallons, but that is not the limit of the leak. The tank is connected to your supply line, so when the bottom rusts through it refills as fast as it empties. Nothing stops the flow except shutting the cold water valve. How bad it gets depends on placement, whether that is the garage, a closet in the hall or an attic sitting above finished rooms, which is the worst.

Our Sioux Falls team extracts the water, then looks at what surrounded the heater. The platform beneath the tank and the base of the closet walls are usually soaked. In a garage, water reaches the wall shared with the house and anything stored on the floor. Attic leaks travel downward through the insulation and the ceilings below. Each of those areas is metered, then dried using dehumidifiers and air movers.

First Five Minutes

Before We Arrive

  1. Cut the Power or GasElectric units get shut down at the breaker. With a gas heater, the knob on the control goes to the off position. Never leave a heating element or burner running on an empty tank.
  2. Close the Cold SupplyTurn the valve on the cold pipe above the heater clockwise. A stuck or leaking valve means going to the house main and shutting the water off there.
  3. Watch for Hot WaterWater from the tank can scald. Keep children and pets away, and do not wade through pooled water until it has had time to cool.
  4. Drain the Tank SafelyIf you can reach the drain valve, attach a garden hose and run it outside or to a floor drain so the remaining water does not empty onto the floor.
  5. Move Stored ItemsGet rugs, boxes and other absorbent things up off the wet floor. Because cardboard and fabric take in water quickly, they hold dampness against the walls and the slab.
The Usual Culprits

How It Usually Starts

  • The tank rusting from within after its anode rod has worn away.
  • Water discharged when the pressure and temperature relief valve fails or opens.
  • An overflow pan that cannot drain, because its line is clogged or the pan itself has rusted.
  • A drain valve that loosens, cracks or no longer closes all the way.
  • Connections that were not fully tightened during a repair or a new installation.
What Is at Risk

What Happens if You Wait

Attic and Ceilings

When an attic heater leaks, water passes through the insulation and lands on the ceiling underneath. Insulation that is wet no longer insulates, and the added weight can pull the ceiling drywall down.

Flooring in Nearby Rooms

From a heater closet, the leak slips beneath the door and reaches hallway hardwood, laminate or carpet. Heat makes it worse, with quicker swelling of wood boards and carpet backing that comes apart.

Garage Wall Base

As water runs across the garage slab, it wets the drywall and bottom plate of the wall the garage shares with the house and continues into flooring on the other side.

Platform and Subfloor

More water goes into the subfloor or wooden stand beneath the tank than anywhere else. Rot can weaken it, a real concern once a full new tank sits on top.

How We Fix It

What Happens After You Call

1

Remove Standing Water

Standing water is taken up from the garage, closet or floor. With an attic unit we also clear the ceiling cavity and overflow pan, which keeps nearby materials from soaking up more.

2

Check Around the Heater

We go over the wall bases, the platform, any ceiling underneath and the adjoining rooms with thermal imaging and moisture meters, which shows how far the wet area extends.

3

Remove Wet Material

We take out baseboards that are damaged, drywall that is crumbling and insulation that is soaked. In attics we clear wet insulation so the ceiling joists and drywall below can be dried.

4

Dry and Monitor

Drying usually takes 3 to 5 days, and dehumidifiers and air movers run the whole time. Our crew logs the readings and lets you know as soon as the area is ready for a replacement heater.

Insurance

Insurance and Water Heater Leak Cleanup

A sudden tank failure is often treated as accidental and sudden, so the resulting damage to belongings, floors and walls is often covered. The water heater itself usually is not, since wearing out with age is considered maintenance. Rust trails, old staining or other evidence that the leak was long-term can lead an adjuster to reduce or deny the claim, since gradual leaks usually fall outside coverage. Check your policy, photograph the heater and the damage, and keep the old unit until the claim is settled.
