Toilet Overflow Cleanup Help in Sioux Falls

An overflowing toilet seems like something towels can handle, except the water keeps moving past the tile. It runs under the baseboard, into the wall and through the gap around the drain pipe. Most important is what the water was carrying. If it came from the tank or the supply line, it is clean. Bowl water is not clean, so we handle it as contaminated from the start.

The first thing our Sioux Falls team does is identify the water category, since every later step depends on it. Clean water means extraction and drying. Gray or black water means protective gear, removal of soaked porous materials like carpet pad, and disinfection of every surface it touched. Finally we meter the subfloor, the bottoms of the walls and the space underneath, so no moisture gets left behind.

First Five Minutes

Before We Arrive

  1. Stop the WaterFind the shutoff valve behind the toilet and turn it clockwise as far as it goes. If it will not turn, lift the tank lid and raise the float to stop the fill.
  2. Do Not Flush AgainDo not flush again or run sinks, showers or the washer. If the drain line is blocked, any water you send down can come back up.
  3. Avoid Dirty WaterIf the water came from the bowl or contains waste, keep children and pets out, and wear gloves and waterproof shoes if you must walk through it.
  4. Look at the Ceiling UnderneathWith a second floor bathroom, inspect the ceiling of the room beneath. A ceiling that sags or drips means turning off that room's power and moving your things clear.
  5. Contain Clean WaterOld towels can stop clean water before it gets to a hallway or carpet. Contaminated water should be left to a crew that has the right disinfectants and equipment.
Coverage

What About Insurance?

A sudden and accidental overflow, for example from an unexpected clog or a fill valve that failed, is covered by many homeowners policies. Unless a backup endorsement was added, policies often exclude water backing up out of a drain or the sewer, which may include an overflow that a blocked main line caused. Damage from a toilet that leaked at the base for months is usually not covered. Read your policy or call your agent to find out which situation applies to you.

Why It Happens

What Causes It

  • Blocked traps and drain lines, which leave the bowl nowhere to empty but onto the floor.
  • A sewer line narrowed by tree roots or a collapse, so every drain in the home runs slow.
  • Failed wax rings under the toilet, which leak at the floor on each flush.
  • Waste rising out of the lowest toilet in the house because the main sewer line is blocked.
  • Tank parts, the fill valve or the flapper, that jam and let water run nonstop.

What the Water Damages

Wet Vanity and Wall Bases

The bottom of the drywall and the toe kick of the vanity take in water and pull it upward. The wall can be wet several inches above the floor while the paint still looks fine.

Odor That Returns

When contaminated water dries under flooring or in the base of a wall, the smell comes back in humid weather and stays until that material is removed.

Subfloor Saturation

Through the grout and around the toilet base, water works its way below tile or vinyl flooring. Plywood below swells and delaminates, which can release the flooring glued to it.

Bacterial Contamination

Bowl and drain water leaves bacteria in carpet, pad, baseboards and drywall. Because disinfecting porous material is not reliable, it usually has to come out.

What We Do

How We Handle Toilet Overflow Cleanup

1

Determine the Water Type

First we determine if the source was the bowl and drain or the supply side. Proper containment and protection are put in place before cleanup starts.

2

Extraction and Removal

Standing water is extracted first. Contaminated drywall, baseboards or carpet pad come out where necessary, and the toilet is lifted when water has reached beneath the flange and base.

3

Clean and Disinfect

Floors, wall bases, fixtures and the subfloor are cleaned and treated with antimicrobial products. When the water is Category 3, we finish this step before turning on any drying equipment.

4

Dry and Verify

Air movers and dehumidifiers dry the bathroom and any affected room below. Moisture readings continue until the ceiling and subfloor return to normal levels.
