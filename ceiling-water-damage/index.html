Ceiling Water Damage Repair: What Sioux Falls Homeowners Should Know

The stain overhead comes at the end of the story, not the beginning. Before any mark shows, water has traveled down a joist, saturated the insulation and collected on top of the drywall. It could come from an upstairs bathroom, plumbing inside the ceiling, an AC condensate line or the roof. Our team finds where it starts before any patching is done.

A coat of paint over the spot solves nothing. While the space overhead stays damp, the stain returns, and within 24 to 48 hours mold can begin on the drywall paper. We open only what we need to, pull out wet insulation, dry the joists and remaining drywall, then patch, texture and paint so the repair blends in. A sagging ceiling is a different matter. Do not stand beneath it, and get help on the way.

What It Looks Like

  • Ceiling paint lifting off in blisters or peeling strips
  • Water dripping from a light fixture, vent or smoke detector
  • Small black or green specks appearing in a cluster overhead
  • Drip marks at the top of a wall where it meets the ceiling
  • Joint tape coming loose, with screws and panel edges telegraphing through
  • Tan or rust colored circles that widen following a storm or a shower
If Nothing Is Done

The Damage Timeline

First hours

Hidden Travel Overhead

Water runs along joists and soaks into insulation. From inside the room everything can appear normal, since the drywall is taking on water on the hidden face.

Hours 24 to 48

Stains and Bubbles

A stain forms and paint starts to blister. Mold can begin on the drywall paper and the wet insulation presses down on the panel.

Days 3 through 7

Sagging Sets In

The panel turns soft and starts slipping off its fasteners. The ceiling bows between joists, seams open up and a musty odor reaches the room.

After a week

Risk of Collapse

Parts of the ceiling can come down along with the wet insulation resting on them. By now mold has settled into the space above, and the area needing repair has grown considerably.

What You Cannot See

What Hides Behind It

Growth on the Hidden Side

Mold usually grows out of sight on the upper face of the panel and in the insulation above, both of which hold moisture well after the painted side dries.

What the Upstairs Is Hiding

A wet ceiling usually means a wet subfloor or attic deck directly over it. We check that level as well, because drying only the ceiling leaves moisture behind.

Electricity Overhead

The damp area usually includes fan mounts, recessed fixtures and wiring boxes. Turn off that room's breaker, because a wet fixture may short out.

The Leak Is Somewhere Else

Because moisture runs along framing, plumbing and ductwork, the drip may be feet away from the entry point. Cutting in just under the mark may leave damp areas elsewhere untouched.

How We Fix It

Our Ceiling Water Damage Repair Process

1

Trace the Leak

We track the water back to where it starts using moisture meters and thermal imaging, checking the room or attic above. Until the water stops coming, fixing the ceiling would not last.

2

Secure the Room

Power to wet fixtures is shut off and furniture below is covered or moved. If the ceiling is holding water, we release it in a controlled way and catch it.

3

Remove and Dry Out

We take down the ruined drywall and the soaked insulation. The joists and nearby ceiling are dried with air movers plus a dehumidifier, and antimicrobial goes on wherever the water was dirty.

4

Rebuild and Paint

Once moisture readings are normal we install new insulation and drywall, tape and finish the seams, match the existing texture and prime with a stain blocker before painting.
