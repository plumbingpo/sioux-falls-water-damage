Crawl Space Water Removal in Sioux Falls

Few homeowners notice right away when water collects beneath the floor. It tends to turn up as a damp chill underfoot, a stale smell at the vents, or a small lake discovered the day somebody lifts the hatch. At that point the batts are typically saturated and the framing overhead has sat damp for several days. Our team pumps the water out, clears what is ruined and dries the framing before it softens.

Once the standing water is gone, the job is only halfway done. Wood framing takes water in gradually and gives it up just as gradually, which is why we meter the joists, beams and subfloor and leave dehumidifiers on until readings drop, typically 3 to 5 days. Our team traces the source too, whether that is a supply line leak, a drain, or rain driven groundwater, so the space stays dry afterward.

Signs to Look For

  • Fuzzy patches, pale or dark, spreading across the framing or subfloor
  • Hardwood boards upstairs starting to cup, with edges higher than the centers
  • Sweating pipes and ductwork that drip onto the ground beneath the home
  • More crickets, roaches or other bugs showing up near the floor level
  • Stale, mildew type air coming up around the vents in the floor
  • Puddles or wet muck visible just inside the crawl space door
Behind the Surface

The Damage You Do Not See

Weakened Floor Framing

Rot often starts on top of the joist or at the sill plate, where you cannot see it from the hatch. Checking with a probe is how we catch sections that have gone soft.

Shifting Piers and Supports

When the ground stays soaked, footings may lose support and piers can tilt or sink. You might notice it as a door that suddenly rubs or a floor that is no longer level.

Pests Drawn to Moisture

Damp wood and wet soil are exactly what termites, carpenter ants and rodents look for. Once the area is dry it becomes far less inviting.

Water That Is Not Clean

Do not assume the water below your floor is clean. If it came from a drain pipe or from waste laden groundwater it is rated Category 2 or 3, so we suit up and disinfect.

Our Process

How We Handle Crawl Space Water Removal

1

Safety Check and Inspection

Nobody enters until the space has been screened for shock risks, hanging ducts and contamination. Then we map how far the water reached and take moisture readings on the joists and subfloor.

2

Pump Out Water

We run pumps and extraction lines until the pooled water is gone, low pockets and trenches along the footings included. Any silt or mud sitting on the liner gets removed during this step.

3

Remove Wet Materials

Soaked insulation, ruined vapor barrier and debris are bagged and carried out. Our crew then wipes down the uncovered framing and applies antimicrobial to spots with growth or a high chance of it.

4

Dry and Verify

Low profile air movers and a dehumidifier run under the house until the wood reaches a normal moisture reading. We recheck with a meter and give you the readings and a prevention plan.

Why Speed Matters

The Damage Timeline

Day one

Puddles Form Underneath

The dirt floor starts absorbing moisture as puddles form in every dip. Batts begin drawing moisture upward and the air below the house turns humid quickly.

Hours 24 to 48

Early Mold Growth

Paper faced insulation and moist joists are where mold is able to appear first. Soaked insulation gains weight and starts to sag off the floor above.

Days 3 through 7

Dampness Rises Indoors

Humid air from below works its way up into the rooms. The lumber goes on taking in moisture, a stale smell spreads and flooring can begin to cup.

Past one week

Wood Starts Rotting

Wood that stays wet begins to decay. Sill plates and joists lose firmness, nails and hangers corrode, and termites along with other pests move toward the moisture.

Coverage varies with the cause. Policies often respond to abrupt accidents, for example a water line breaking under the home. Standard policies generally exclude groundwater, flooding from outside and leaks that dripped for months. Check your policy or call your agent, and we can document the damage with photos and moisture readings for your claim.
Moist ground after a downpour is nothing unusual, though actual puddles are not something to leave alone. Just an inch or two will hold humidity at a level where mold grows and lumber rots. If water is still there a day after the rain stops, or shows up in dry weather, have it checked.
The pump out itself is normally done in a few hours. Expect 3 to 5 days after that for the lumber, with dehumidification and air movement going the whole time. Heavy timbers, bare soil without a vapor barrier, or weeks of standing water may stretch that out. We go by moisture readings, not by the calendar.
Usually it does need to go. Fiberglass batts hold water like a sponge, lose their insulating value and keep the subfloor above them wet. They also sag and fall once they are heavy. Our crew pulls the soaked sections to let the framing dry, then new batts are installed after the wood meters normal again.
Small puddles of clean water are within reach of a shop vac. Joists and subfloor stay wet, though, since a box fan only pushes moist air in circles. The space is cramped and dark as well, with a chance of live wires or sewage down there. Anything beyond a shallow puddle is better pumped out and dried by a crew with the right gear.
